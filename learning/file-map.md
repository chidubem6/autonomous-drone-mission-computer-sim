since 2026-09-17 tick() also takes the target waypoint, so the drone steers onto the bearing, moves along it at cruise speed, and climbs to that waypoint's own altitude; since 2026-09-20 it also flies the mission to its end — an arrival radius decides when a waypoint counts as reached, current_wp advances, each arrival is announced, and the loop breaks with MISSION COMPLETE rather than indexing past the array → [[arrival-threshold]], [[loop-control-break]], [[off-by-one-errors]], [[undefined-behaviour]], [[truthiness-in-c]]; - src/main.c — known (2026-09-21) — the mission program: the decisions. Every one of those is a choice, and none is a fact about drones — which is why they are here and not in the engine → [[engine-vs-program-separation]], [[main-loop]], [[arrays-of-structs]], [[waypoint-list]]; since 2026-09-25 it owns everything the terminal sees: print_state lives here, and mode_to_string turns a FlightMode into its name so a mode change can be announced by comparing the mode before and after each tick → [[switch-statement]], [[string-literals]], [[data-contract]]; since 2026-09-25 a comment above print_state points at docs/telemetry-contract.md. - tests/ — known (2026-09-21) — the test programs: code that makes claims about the engine and checks them. One file for now; it splits by aspect the day running a subset actually saves time → [[test-program]]
- tests/test_drone.c — known (2026-09-22) — claims about the engine, written as asserts. Links the REAL engine, never a copy, so it cannot go stale behind a bugfix. Claim 1: after a tick, the battery is lower than it was. Claim 2 (2026-09-22): flying east at 10 m/s for one second lands the drone at x = 10, and leaves y alone — set up as its OWN drone and waypoint rather than reusing claim 1's, with the waypoint deliberately far past the answer so no engine can pass by snapping to the target. Both claims compare with fabs(actual - expected) < TOLERANCE_M rather than ==, because binary rounding put 6.1e-16 m of phantom drift into y. Every claim here has been proved fair by breaking the engine and watching it abort. Since 2026-09-24 each claim is its own function named after what it claims, so a failure prints the claim's name instead of `main:`, names like `d` and `w` can be reused, and nothing in main() is visible to any claim. Claims 3-8 test the engine at its forks: a dead drone (battery exactly 0) sinks; a healthy drone climbs toward a higher target, descends toward a lower one, and holds exactly at its target; and inside one step of the target the climb and descent caps land it exactly (checked with ==, because exactness is the claim), with the takeoff→navigate transition asserted at that same pivot → [[assert]], [[test-program]], [[compile-time-vs-run-time]], [[test-fairness]], [[test-isolation]], [[nan-and-float-comparison]], [[choosing-a-tolerance]], [[named-constants]], [[edge-cases]], [[variable-scope]], [[tests-check-behaviour-not-code]]; since 2026-09-24 claims 9-10 check that an airborne drone whose battery dies goes to MODE_FAILSAFE, from navigating and from taking off. Both were written and seen red BEFORE the engine change → [[test-first-red-green]], [[regression-testing]]; since 2026-09-25 there is no assert in it: a check() helper prints each check's description with PASS or FAIL in one column and carries on, every claim returns how many of its checks failed, and main() adds them up and exits 1 if any did. Every claim runs on every run, and make, or a build server, learns the verdict from the exit status. Every claim also sets its drone's .mode explicitly rather than leaning on MODE_TAKEOFF being 0 → [[test-helper-function]], [[exit-status]], [[zero-initialization]]
